Grand Theft Auto VI finally came out on November 19, 2026, and if you're the kind of person who reads a car blog, you've probably spent more time staring at the traffic in Vice City than doing missions. The game is set in Rockstar's version of Florida, and Florida means old American iron: chrome bumpers, vinyl tops, four-door sedans with big engines and bad paint.

None of the cars in GTA have real names. Rockstar invents its own brands (Vapid instead of Ford, Bravado instead of Dodge, Declasse instead of Chevy) and it never says which real car a model is based on. But anyone who knows cars can see where most of them came from, and that's half the fun.

Here are the muscle cars from GTA 6 that are easiest to match to the real thing, what the real cars actually were, and what they make (or made) in the real world.

A quick ground rule: the in-game cars below all showed up in Rockstar's official trailers or promo shots and in post-launch car lists. The real-car matches are how fans and car guides read them. Rockstar hasn't confirmed any of them, so treat "looks inspired by" as exactly that.

## The quick list

| GTA 6 car | Looks inspired by | Where it showed up |
|---|---|---|
| Vapid Dominator (and Dominator '67) | Ford Mustang (modern and first-gen) | First trailer, post-launch car lists |
| Bravado Gauntlet Classic | 1970s Dodge Challenger | First trailer |

## Vapid Dominator vs. Ford Mustang

The Dominator has been GTA's Mustang for years, and it's back in GTA 6 in several flavors. Car lists match the base Dominator to the Mustang, the Dominator GT to the Mustang convertible, and the Dominator '67 to the first-generation car. There's even a Dominator '67 Buggy in the Ultimate Edition, which is a very GTA thing to do to a classic pony car.

![Teal classic Ford Mustang parked on the side of a road](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1540929562752-e789ec6239d0?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)
*A classic first-generation Ford Mustang. Photo: [Mike Von](https://unsplash.com/photos/teal-classic-ford-mustang-parked-on-side-of-road-YBEyahCAcHU) on Unsplash*

**The real car.** Henry Ford II unveiled the Mustang at the World's Fair in New York on April 17, 1964, at a starting price of around $2,300. According to History.com, nearly 22,000 were snapped up on day one and Ford sold more than 400,000 in the first year. The hot early engine was the K-code "Hi-Po" 289 V8, rated at 271 hp and 312 lb-ft, and it only came with a four-speed manual.

The modern Mustang is a different animal. Ford's 2026 spec sheet rates the GT's 5.0-liter Coyote V8 at 480 hp (486 hp with the optional active-valve exhaust), and the Dark Horse at 500 hp. The base 2.3-liter EcoBoost makes 315 hp. So a new Mustang GT, the car you can rent at the Miami airport, makes more power than a 1970 Hemi was rated at.

## Bravado Gauntlet Classic vs. Dodge Challenger

Bravado is GTA's Dodge, and the Gauntlet is its Challenger. GTA 6 has several Gauntlets, including the Gauntlet Classic and the newer-looking Gauntlet Hellfire, both seen in the trailers. The Classic is the one car guides match to the original Challenger.

![Orange vintage Dodge Challenger on a gravel road](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1604940500627-d3f44d1d21c6?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)
*A 1970s Dodge Challenger. Photo: [Alexandros Athanasopoulos](https://unsplash.com/photos/orange-dodge-challenger-on-gravel-road-GUFR1WsgCQQ) on Unsplash*

**The real car.** The first-generation Challenger ran from 1970 to 1974. Dodge built it on Chrysler's new E-body, the same basic unit body as the Plymouth Barracuda, and it was late to the pony-car party. It still sold well: more than 80,000 in 1970, beating the Barracuda.

The engine list is the stuff of legend. The 426 Hemi was rated at 425 gross horsepower and the 440 Six Pack (three two-barrel carbs) at 390 gross. Those are 1970 gross ratings, measured with no accessories, so they read higher than a modern net number would. Even so, a Hemi Challenger was a serious car then and is a very expensive one now.

## Declasse Tulip vs. Chevrolet Chevelle

This is the one a lot of people noticed first. The Tulip is the four-door muscle sedan Jason and Lucia drive at the end of the second trailer, and fans identify it as an early-'70s Chevelle Malibu. It's a smart pick for the setting: a four-door Chevelle is exactly the kind of car that would still be cruising around South Florida, slightly faded, with somebody's cousin behind the wheel. There's also a two-door Tulip M100 in Rockstar's promo shots.

![Red and black 1970 Chevrolet Chevelle at a car show](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1726585627566-045719072563?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)
*A 1970 Chevrolet Chevelle. Photo: [Zoshua Colah](https://unsplash.com/photos/a-red-and-black-car-parked-in-a-parking-lot-HG1S10AWFpM) on Unsplash*

**The real car.** The Chevelle was Chevy's midsize car, and the Super Sport versions are the ones everybody remembers. The king was the 1970 SS 454 with the LS6 engine, rated at 450 hp and 500 lb-ft. The milder LS5 454 that year was rated at 360 hp with the same 500 lb-ft. Both are gross ratings, the same caveat as the Challenger.

The four-door Malibu the Tulip seems to be based on was never the hero car. Most were family sedans with small-block V8s, which is exactly why the game's choice feels right. GTA has always been about the cars real people drove, not just the posters on the wall.

## Bravado Buffalo STX vs. Dodge Charger

The Buffalo is GTA's modern Charger, and the Buffalo STX is the muscle-class version seen in the first trailer. In a game set around Miami, the cop-car-turned-street-car Charger is a natural fit.

![Black Dodge Charger at night with its taillights on](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1561781884-ecd5a0af060e?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)
*A Dodge Charger at night. Photo: [Agreen](https://unsplash.com/photos/black-dodge-charger-with-taillights-on-OJ1Up9D0Z3c) on Unsplash*

**The real car.** The modern Charger was a four-door sedan with a V8 option, which made it a rare thing: a muscle car with room for the whole family. The supercharged SRT Hellcat arrived in Dodge's lineup in 2015 at 707 hp, and the Charger Hellcats ranged from 717 hp up to 807 hp in Redeye form, according to Kelley Blue Book. The 2023 model year was the last for the Hellcats and the V8 Charger sedan.

## Imponte Phoenix vs. Pontiac Firebird

Imponte is GTA's Pontiac, and the Phoenix has shown up in both trailers. Car guides list it as a Pontiac muscle car, and fans have long read it as a second-generation Firebird, the long-nosed shape that ran through the '70s.

![Black 1978 Pontiac Firebird](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1560343757-9407710de8a6?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)
*A 1978 Pontiac Firebird. Photo: [Ali Moharami](https://unsplash.com/photos/black-pontiac-firebird-KnSiiBnaIzA) on Unsplash*

**The real car.** The second-gen Firebird is best known in Trans Am form, and the most famous Trans Am of all is the black-and-gold 1977 Special Edition from *Smokey and the Bandit*. Pontiac built 15,567 Special Edition Trans Ams in 1977, according to Old Cars Weekly.

The specs are a reminder of how far things fell in the '70s. The 1977 Trans Am's engines made between 180 and 200 hp: the Pontiac 400 at 180 hp, the T/A 6.6 at 200 hp, and the Oldsmobile 403 at 185 hp (the 403 was mandatory in California and high-altitude areas). A modern EcoBoost Mustang makes more than 50 percent more power than the best of them. The Bandit didn't need horsepower, though. He had the look.

## A few more worth spotting

GTA 6 is packed with old American metal, and these muscle-class cars are also in the trailers and car lists:

- **Bravado Gauntlet Hellfire**: the modern, angry Gauntlet, seen in both trailers.
- **Declasse Vigero**: guides match it to the Chevy Camaro, and Rockstar showed a Vigero ZX convertible in a promo shot.
- **Albany Buccaneer**: a big two-door that car lists tie to the Buick Riviera and Ford Fairlane. A lowrider Buccaneer Custom is in the first trailer too.
- **Vapid Stanier '55**: car lists match it to the mid-'50s Ford Fairlane.

## Why GTA's fake cars still matter to real car people

It's easy to roll your eyes at a video game on a car blog, but GTA has been a gateway drug for a lot of enthusiasts. Plenty of people knew the shape of a Gauntlet before they knew what a Challenger was, and then went looking for the real thing. Rockstar clearly gets that. Ahead of launch it reportedly teamed up with West Coast Customs on real-life builds of six GTA 6 cars ahead of launch.

The best part is that most of the real cars on this list are still out there. A first-gen Mustang, a '70s Chevelle sedan or a second-gen Firebird is a much more realistic project than a Hemi Challenger. If one of these got its hooks in you while you were cruising Vice City, start with our guide to the [best project cars for beginners](/best-project-cars-for-beginners/) and see what a real one would take.

Just don't drive it like you're in the game.
