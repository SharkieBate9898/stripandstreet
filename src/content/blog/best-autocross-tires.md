Tires are the biggest upgrade you can make for autocross. A stock Miata on great tires will beat a modified Miata on worn-out all-seasons almost every time.

This guide covers the best **200-treadwear** tires for autocross right now, who each one is for, and what they cost. 200-treadwear is the stickiest street-legal performance tire category and the minimum for many autocross classes.

## Quick picks

## 1. Falken Azenis RT660+: best overall

The RT660+ is the default answer for most drivers. Owners and testers report:

- **Very low heat sensitivity.** It keeps gripping on hot days and back-to-back runs.
- **Long life.** Owner logs suggest four to five autocross seasons, which makes it the longest-lasting tire in this group.
- **Great value.** It's priced below most of its rivals and lasts longer.

**The trade-off:** on a cool morning, it gives up a little peak grip to the Yokohama A052.

**Get it if:** you want one tire that works in almost any weather, lasts and doesn't need babying.

## 2. Yokohama Advan A052: fastest when it's cool

When conditions are right, the A052 is the grippiest tire here. Comparison testing puts it **0.2–0.5 seconds per run quicker** than the RT660+ in cool weather.

**The trade-offs:**
- **It doesn't love heat.** Performance drops off in hot temperatures (roughly above 85°F), so it's a tough pick in Southern summers.
- **It wears fastest.** Expect around two to three seasons.
- **Limited sizes.** Check that it comes in yours.

**Get it if:** you live somewhere mild, chase every tenth and don't mind buying tires more often.

## 3. Bridgestone Potenza RE-71RS: the all-rounder

The RE-71RS is the steady, well-proven choice. It's not the fastest on a cool, dry day, but nobody calls it a bad tire, and it's the **best of this group in the wet**.

- Reviewers rate it highly for consistency and wet grip.
- It's quicker than the older RE-71R it replaced.
- Expect around **three to four seasons** of autocross.

**The trade-off:** it can be pricey in bigger sizes, and some owners report fast front wear under heavy autocross use.

**Get it if:** your events run rain or shine and you want predictable grip.

## 4. Nankang Sportnex CR-S: the budget pick (dry only)

The CR-S is the value play. Reviewers say it **matches older 200-treadwear tires in the dry** for less money and lasts longer than the A052 and Hoosier.

**The big trade-off:** wet grip. Owners consistently rate it poor in the rain.

**Get it if:** your budget is tight and your events are mostly dry.

## 5. Hoosier TrackAttack Pro: max pace, read the rules

Testers recorded big gains over premium street tires. It's the outright fastest street-legal option in this roundup, but:

- **It's the most expensive** and wears the fastest.
- **It may not be legal in every class.** Some classes have tread depth rules or approved-tire lists. Check your club's rulebook before you buy.

**Get it if:** you're running events where it's allowed and you want the fastest option, cost be damned.

## Should you even buy 200-treadwear tires yet?

**If you've done fewer than three or four events: probably not.** Your first season is about learning the course, your car and how to look ahead. Any safe tire with decent tread works.

Once you're hooked, upgrading to a 200-treadwear tire is the single biggest step up in grip you can buy.

## Tips to get the most out of autocross tires

- **Use a second set of wheels.** Swap them at the event and save your street tires (and your autocross tires) from daily wear.
- **Watch your pressures.** Start around the manufacturer's recommendation, then adjust between runs based on wear at the shoulders. Bring a good [tire pressure gauge](#affiliate).
- **Rotate front to back if your car allows it**, because fronts often wear faster in autocross.
- **Store them properly** in the off-season: clean, out of the sun and away from heat.
- **Check the date code.** Even unused tires harden with age.
